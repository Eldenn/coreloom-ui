# Editor AI

AI inside `RichTextEditor`: select text, press ⌘J / Ctrl+J (or the **AI** button), then type an instruction or pick a quick action — improve, fix spelling and grammar, shorten, lengthen, summarise, translate. Or type `/` at the start of a line and pick **Ask AI…**, **Continue writing** or a quick action from the menu at the cursor. The answer streams into a preview; nothing touches the document until the person chooses **Review changes**, **Replace selection** or **Insert below**. Front-end only: your app calls the model.

**The consumer provides** `ai={{ onRequest }}` on the editor. `onRequest({ action, prompt, language, text, scope, signal })` returns the answer as an `AsyncIterable<string>` of pieces (each appended — the AI SDK's `textStream` fits) or a `Promise<string>`. `text` is the selection as light Markdown, or the whole document when nothing is selected (`scope` says which) — for `"continue"`, the document up to the cursor; `prompt` is the person's instruction when `action` is `"custom"`; `language` is the BCP 47 target of `"translate"`. Optionally:

- `actions` — the quick actions the panel offers, in order (default: all six);
- `slashCommand` — the `/` menu: `false` turns it off; a list of actions picks what follows **Ask AI…** (default: `continue`, then `actions` without `translate`, which needs a language — Ask AI… has it);
- `languages` — what Translate offers (default `en`, `fr`, `es`, `de`), named in the reader's language;
- `defaultOpen` — open the panel on mount, or pass a command (`{ action: "custom", prompt: "Draft release notes" }`) to run it right away.
- `review` — `false` removes **Review changes** and makes Replace the main action again (default `true`).

- Pass `signal` to your fetch or `streamText`: Stop, Escape, closing the panel and Try again all abort it. Stop keeps what was written so far (or, if nothing came yet, goes back to the instruction field).
- `text` and the answer are light Markdown: paragraphs (a line ending in `\` or two spaces is a line break; other line breaks are spaces), `##` headings, `-` and `1.` lists nested by indentation, `>` quotes, code fences, `---`, `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `~~strike~~`, `` `code` ``, `[links](https://…)`, and `<u>underline</u>`. So bold, italic, strike, underline, inline code and links survive a rewrite; a code block's language and a quote's inner structure don't.
- The answer is inserted as text nodes and marks, never parsed as HTML: `<u>` is the one tag read (as underline), any other tag stays text. Links keep only `http`, `https` and `mailto` addresses; any other link keeps its words without the address.
- `*` and `_` mark emphasis only at word edges (stricter than CommonMark), so `MAX_RETRY_COUNT`, `file_name.ts`, `__init__.py`, `src/*/x.ts` and `2*3*4` stay as written. Characters that would read as Markdown are escaped with `\` in `text`.
- One sentence rewritten inside a paragraph stays inline; anything with blocks replaces the selection as blocks. Insert below goes after the block holding the selection (or at the end), and Summarise makes it the default action.
- The selection stays highlighted while the panel is open. Escape closes the panel and puts focus back in the editor, on the same selection. Each change is one undo step.
- If the selected text disappears while the panel is open (the app reloads `value`, a collaborator deletes it), Replace selection, Insert below and Try again turn off and the panel says why; the rest of the document is never touched in its place.
- Errors show in the panel with **Try again**; the message of an `Error` you throw is shown under the generic text, so throw messages meant for people ("The model is busy. Try again in a minute.").
- Screen readers hear "Writing…", then "Suggestion ready." from a polite live region; the preview is a labelled, focusable region; every action is a button in tab order.
- Without `ai` the editor looks and behaves as before: no button, no shortcut. The selection-highlight extension is always registered (it does nothing until the panel opens), so `ai` can be turned on after mount.
- **The `/` menu.** `/` at the start of a line or after a space (not inside a word, a URL, inline code or a code block) opens a list of AI commands under the cursor (above it near the bottom of the window). Typing filters it — by the label in the reader's language or by English words (`/ai` lists everything, Ask AI… first; `/sum`, `/cont`, `/fix`); no match hides it and the text stays as typed. ↑ / ↓ move (and wrap), Enter or Tab choose, Escape closes it and keeps the text; clicking elsewhere, leaving the editor or scrolling the `/` out of view closes it too. Enter or Tab right after a lone `/` (nothing typed after it, arrows not used) is an ordinary key: "Ratio 1 /" then Enter is a new line.
- The menu is a Radix popover anchored at the `/`: it flips above near the bottom of the window and works inside a modal `Dialog` or `Sheet` (it takes clicks, and Escape closes the menu first, the dialog only on the next press).
- Choosing removes the typed `/…` and opens the panel at that spot (a caret marks it). **Ask AI…** focuses the instruction field; an action runs at once and focus follows it (Stop, then the main button). Instructions and quick actions work on the whole document, as with no selection. **Insert here** is the main, focused action: it puts the answer at the `/` spot — in place of its empty line, inline when the answer is one paragraph, otherwise after its block — so Enter never replaces the document by accident; **Replace all** stays available as a second button. **Continue writing** sends the text before the cursor and only offers Insert here (it never replaces anything). Discard or Escape puts the cursor back on that spot, and undo brings the typed `/…` back.
- Screen readers: the editor stays focused (so typing keeps filtering); while the menu is open the textbox gets `aria-autocomplete="list"`, `aria-controls` (the listbox, named "AI commands") and `aria-activedescendant` (the highlighted option), and loses them when it closes. Moving real focus into the list would stop the typing, so this is the combobox pattern on the editor. A polite status says how many commands match.
- Without `ai` (or with `slashCommand: false`), `/` is just a slash. The slash extension is always registered and stays inert until the menu mounts.
- **Review changes.** For a rewrite of existing text (improve, fix, shorten, lengthen, or an instruction) it is the main, focused button; Replace stays next to it. It closes the panel and draws the answer as changes in the text: removed words struck through (`<del>`), new words underlined (`<ins>`), whole blocks when a block was added, removed or changed kind (a paragraph that became a heading). Words are compared in place, so a fixed typo is one small change, not a rewritten paragraph; changes one space apart read as one ("the quick" → "a fast"). Summaries, continuations, answers at the `/` spot and translations (every word changes) don't offer it.
- A bar under the text names the current change ("Change 2 of 4", "Replace “make” with “makes”") with **Previous** / **Next** (they wrap), **Reject**, **Accept**, **Reject all** and **Accept all**. The caret starts on the first change; clicking or moving the caret into a change makes it the current one. Accepting or rejecting moves on to the next one and keeps focus on the button; when the last one goes, focus goes back to the text. Tab from the text reaches the bar.
- Nothing changes in the document (or in `value`) until a change is accepted: each Accept is one undo step, Accept all is one step for all. Undo brings the text back, not the suggestion. Editing the text of a change drops that change; reloading `value` drops them all. A new review replaces the one in progress.
- Screen readers hear how many changes there are when the review starts, then "Change accepted. 3 left." after each one, from one polite live region; the bar is a group named "Suggested changes".
- If the answer is the same as the text, **Review changes** turns off and the panel says there is nothing to review.
- Label AI output as a suggestion and keep the person in charge: never apply an answer without them choosing to.

**In the ⌘K palette:** `CommandMenu` takes `onAskAi(query)`. Once something is typed, "Ask AI: “…”" comes first in the list (and stays when nothing else matches); choosing it closes the palette and hands you the query — open your assistant with it.

**In an app:** `pnpm dlx shadcn@latest add @coreloom/rich-text-editor` (the AI panel is `rich-text-ai.tsx` and the review `rich-text-review.tsx`, installed with it) and `@coreloom/command-menu`.
